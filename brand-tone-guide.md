# SpiceJet Brand Tone & Voice Guide (Prototype)

## [TONE-01] Core Brand Voice Principles
The SpiceJet voice is calm, accountable, empathetic, and human. We communicate in plain, transparent language and never hide behind corporate jargon, robotic boilerplate, or defensive phrasing. When things go wrong, we acknowledge customer frustration immediately and take ownership of guiding the passenger toward an orderly resolution.

Key pillars:
- **Calm & Measured**: De-escalate heated moments with composed, steady reassurance.
- **Accountable**: Own the friction without making unauthorized legal admissions.
- **Empathetic**: Put customer distress first before citing procedures or rules.
- **Direct & Plain**: Use short sentences, active voice, and everyday words. Avoid phrases like "inconvenience caused is deeply regretted" when simple empathy works better.

## [TONE-02] Empathy-First Opening Patterns
Always begin responses to distressed or inconvenienced passengers with genuine empathy before offering next steps. Validate what the passenger is experiencing.

Approved openers:
- "We are truly sorry to hear about your experience with this flight delay."
- "We understand how stressful missing a connection or waiting at the airport is."
- "Thank you for bringing this to our attention. We know your time is valuable."
- "We hear your frustration regarding your baggage, and we want to help locate it as quickly as possible."
- "That is certainly not the standard of service we aim to provide."

Unapproved openers:
- "As per airline terms and conditions..."
- "Please be advised that delays are outside our control..."
- "Dear passenger, we regret any inconvenience..."

## [TONE-03] Do and Don't Guidelines
Follow these clear rules when interacting across all social channels:

**DO:**
- Acknowledge the passenger's specific problem directly (delay, baggage, booking, cancellation).
- Apologize for the inconvenience and frustration caused.
- Direct passengers to official channels (`[OFFICIAL_SUPPORT_CHANNEL]`) for case-specific, private assistance.
- Maintain an encouraging and constructive tone.
- Keep responses within platform character limits.

**DON'T:**
- Do not promise specific refund amounts, compensation sums, or fixed turnaround hours.
- Do not make flight status guarantees without verification from Airport Operations.
- Do not argue, deflect blame to third parties, or blame air traffic control or airport staff.
- Do not ask passengers to publicly share PNR, phone numbers, or email addresses.
- Do not use corporate cliches or sarcastic retorts.

## [TONE-04] Platform-Specific Tone Adaptations
Adjust response style to fit the conversational norms of each social platform while preserving brand voice:

- **X (formerly Twitter)** (Limit: 280 chars): Crisp, concise, fast-moving, and direct. Empathize in one clause, state the immediate channel, and move private details to direct message or official support portal.
- **Instagram** (Limit: 500 chars): Warm, supportive, conversational, and visually thoughtful. Express care, keep formatting readable, and guide user to private support.
- **YouTube** (Limit: 500 chars): Informative, structured, patient, and polite. Address community comments constructively with clear reference to customer service channels.
- **Reddit** (Limit: 800 chars): Authentic, conversational, transparent, and completely free of canned PR talk. Redittors detect corporate deflection instantly; speak plainly and explain support processes frankly.

## [TONE-05] Responding to Hinglish Posts
Many passengers communicate in Hinglish (Hindi written in Roman script) or colloquial Indian English.
topics: Customer Service, Flight Delay, Baggage Issue, Refund

Guidelines:
- Match the customer's linguistic comfort by responding in respectful, natural Roman-script Hinglish or warm, accessible Indian English.
- Avoid stiff, overly formal British English when the customer is speaking informally in Hinglish.
- Never mock, correct, or mimic customer slang pejoratively.
- Hinglish examples:
  - Customer: "Bhai flight ka update kab milega? Gate pe koi bata hi nahi raha."
  - Recommended Response: "Aapki pareshani hum samajh sakte hain. Flight updates ke liye hamari ground team se coordination jaari hai. Kripya apna PNR hume [OFFICIAL_SUPPORT_CHANNEL] par share karein taaki hum turant madad kar sakein."

## [TONE-06] Handling Sarcasm and Irony
Customers frequently use irony, mocking emojis (🙃), or sarcastic praise (e.g., "truly an on-time champion") to express deep disappointment.
topics: Flight Delay, Flight Cancellation, Baggage Issue

Rules:
- Never mirror sarcasm, make jokes, or use clever comebacks.
- Sarcastic praise about a failure is a **Negative** complaint that signals serious customer distress.
- Strip away the irony and address the root issue directly and respectfully.
- Example:
  - Customer: "Wow, only three hours late. Truly an on-time champion 🙃"
  - Weak reply: "Thank you for calling us a champion!" (Avoid completely)
  - Strong reply: "We completely understand your frustration with this three-hour delay, and we apologize for upsetting your schedule. Our airport team is working to get your flight boarded as quickly and safely as possible. Please check [OFFICIAL_SUPPORT_CHANNEL] for live updates."

## [TONE-07] Acknowledging Positive Feedback and Compliments
When passengers share positive experiences or praise our cabin crew and ground staff:
topics: Positive Experience, Customer Service

- Respond promptly with genuine warmth and gratitude.
- Acknowledge specific crew members or airport teams mentioned in the post.
- Keep the response enthusiastic without being excessively dramatic.
- Example: "Thank you so much for your kind words! We will be sure to pass on your appreciation to our cabin crew. We look forward to welcoming you aboard SpiceJet again soon!"

## [TONE-08] Emoji Policy
- Use emojis sparingly and only where they enhance warmth or clarity (e.g., 🙏, ✈️).
- Never use laughing, rolling eyes, or playful emojis on complaint or delay threads.
- For neutral or positive interactions, a polite flight or folded hands emoji is acceptable.
- On negative or crisis posts, avoid decorative emojis entirely to ensure tone remains serious and attentive.

## [TONE-09] Examples of Weak vs. Strong Drafts
- **Delay Complaint**:
  - *Weak*: "Dear user, delay is regretted due to operational reasons. Pls bear with us."
  - *Strong*: "We are deeply sorry for the delay and understand how frustrating waiting without clear news can be. Our station team is coordinating departure details, and we invite you to check [OFFICIAL_SUPPORT_CHANNEL] for live status assistance."
- **Baggage Not Arrived**:
  - *Weak*: "File PIR at counter. Bag will come soon."
  - *Strong*: "We understand how concerning it is when your bag does not appear on the belt. If you are still at the terminal, please speak with our baggage desk to register a report, or share details with us at [OFFICIAL_SUPPORT_CHANNEL] so our team can track it."
